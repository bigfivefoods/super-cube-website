# Super-Cube® LMS: benchmark research and design rules

Prepared 7 October 2026 (SAST) for Dr Craig R. Muller. This compares how world-leading assessment instruments and learning products work, then turns that into concrete rules for the Super-Cube® LMS. ## 1. What the leaders do

### 1.1 Assessment instruments

| Instrument | What it does well | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Gallup CliftonStrengths** | 200 paired statements (the older StrengthsFinder 2.0 had 177) with a 20-second limit per pair to get top-of-mind answers; the timer can be switched off for people with reading difficulties. Paired comparisons of two socially desirable statements reduce faking compared with single-statement Likert items. Reports are strengths-first and explicitly *not* for selection or mental-health screening. Progress auto-saves. | Gallup, *CliftonStrengths Technical Report* (2023); Gallup Help Center, "How is the CliftonStrengths assessment structured?" |
| **VIA Inventory of Strengths** | 24 character strengths. The adult short form **VIA-IS-M** (96 items) mixes positively and negatively keyed items (2 + 2 per strength) and VIA recommends it when acquiescence ("yea-saying") or careless responding matter; the **VIA-IS-P** is all-positive. Separate **youth forms** (VIA Youth-96 for ages 10–17; VIA Youth-103 for 13–17) with simpler language, and a youth spirituality scale written to be secular (no mention of religion or faith). | McGrath (2019), *VIA Assessment Suite for Adults technical report*; viacharacter.org youth instrument pages |
| **EQ-i 2.0 / EQ 360 2.0 (MHS)** | 133 items: 15 subscales in 5 composites, a well-being indicator and validity scales. **Validity is checked before interpretation**: time to completion, an Inconsistency Index (10 pairs of near-identical items), Positive/Negative Impression items, a final honesty item ("My responses … were open and honest") and omitted-item rate. The 360 version compares self with rater groups and treats gaps of more than 10 points as meaningful. Negative-impression items were rewritten to avoid clinical connotations. | MHS EQ-i 2.0 User's Handbook, Part IV "Understanding the Results" and Part V "Stages of Development" |
| **MSCEIT V2.0** | An *ability* test of emotional intelligence (141 items, four branches: perceiving, using, understanding, managing emotions). Answers are scored against **consensus** (share of a norm group choosing each answer) or **expert** keys; the two keys give very similar scores. This is the logic behind scoring SJT options by expert agreement. | Mayer, Salovey, Caruso & Sitarenios (2003), *Emotion* 3(1); MSCEIT V2.0 manual summaries |
| **Hogan (HPI, HDS, MVPI, Hogan 360)** | Separates "bright side" (HPI, everyday performance), "dark side" (HDS: 11 derailers that show up under stress, each with behavioural subscales) and values (MVPI). Development feedback names *overused strengths* as risks, not just low scores. Hogan 360 is a 50-item multi-rater with a global benchmark and open comments. | hoganassessments.com (HDS); JMU *Leadership Instruments Library* 2.4.1 |
| **CCL Benchmarks® 360** | Benchmarks for Managers™ measures 16 competencies and 5 career derailers, rated by boss, superiors, peers, direct reports and others. **Peer and direct-report ratings are only shown combined** so raters stay anonymous; boss ratings may be identifiable. Over-raters (self higher than others) are at greater derailment risk than under-raters, and peer ratings are the best derailment predictor. | ccl.org Benchmarks 360 page; CCL Resources "Rating a colleague", "Rater categories", "Benchmarks for Managers scoring rules matrix"; CCL "Understanding your 360 results" |

### 1.2 Learning products

| Product | Pattern worth copying | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Harvard ManageMentor** | Short, topic-based modules for managers, each with a core idea, practical steps, tools/worksheets and a check. Built for "learning in the flow of work" rather than long courses. | Harvard Business Publishing Corporate Learning product pages |
| **Duolingo** | Bite-size daily lessons, streaks, and **spaced repetition** driven by a trained model of memory decay (half-life regression), so items come back just before you would forget them. | Settles & Meeder (2016), "A Trainable Spaced Repetition Model for Language Learning", ACL |
| **Headspace / Calm** | One short guided session a day, a clear "today" action, gentle reminders, and honest, non-clinical framing with signposting to professional help. | Product observation |

### 1.3 Learning science that should shape every session

* **Retrieval practice and spacing.** Practice testing and distributed practice are the two techniques rated "high utility" across ages and materials (Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham, 2013, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1)). Recalling an idea beats re-reading it (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, *Psychological Science* 17(3)).
* **Implementation intentions ("if–then" plans).** A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests found a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment, d = 0.65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38). The form is: *"If [situation], then I will [behaviour]."*
* **WOOP / MCII (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan).** Mental contrasting plus an if–then plan. In a randomised study with high-school students, a 30-minute written MCII exercise increased practice-test effort compared with a control task (Duckworth, Grant, Loew, Oettingen & Gollwitzer, 2011, *Educational Psychology* 31(1)).
* **Feedback on quiz answers.** Explaining *why* an answer is right (or not) turns a check into learning, not just measurement.

### 1.4 Item-writing and psychometric practice

* **Balanced keying, done carefully.** Mixing positively and reverse-keyed items helps detect and offset acquiescence (VIA-IS-M rationale above). But *negated* items ("I do not…") confuse respondents and add error (van Sonderen, Sanderman & Coyne, 2013, *PLOS ONE* 8(7): e68967). **Rule: write reverse-keyed items as a positive description of the opposite behaviour** ("I put off hard decisions until someone else makes them"), never with "not".
* **Behavioural anchors.** Behaviourally anchored rating scales (Smith & Kendall, 1963, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 47(2)) describe observable actions, not traits or self-labels. **Rule: each item describes something a person *does* in a situation, ideally with a frequency, not "I am good at X".**
* **Situational judgement tests (SJTs).** SJTs present a realistic situation and response options. Knowledge instructions ("Which is the most effective response?") correlate more with cognitive ability; behavioural-tendency instructions ("What would you most likely do?") correlate more with personality; both predict performance and add validity beyond cognitive ability and the Big Five (McDaniel, Hartman, Whetzel & Grubb, 2007, *Personnel Psychology* 60(1)). **Rule: use "most effective" instructions (harder to fake), and score each option on a 1–4 effectiveness key agreed by experts, as MSCEIT does with expert keys.**
* **Children and Likert scales.** Children aged 6–13 handle word-based scales better than numbers; frequency words (never → always) work best; abstract statements are hard; corroborate with parents/teachers (Mellor & Moore, 2014, *Journal of Pediatric Psychology* 39(3)). **Rule: the Kids form uses short concrete sentences, a word-labelled frequency scale, and very few reverse-keyed items, all without negation.**
* **Validity checks before interpretation** (EQ-i 2.0 model): attention item, straight-lining, speed, and an honesty item. Super-Cube® already has the first three from Phase 1 stage 2.
* **Observer forms / 360.** Parallel wording in the third person, rater groups (manager, peer, direct report), **minimum of 3 raters before any group is shown** (CCL combines peers and direct reports for anonymity), and self–other gaps highlighted.
* **Reliability and validity steps** (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*): pilot → item analysis (corrected item–total r ≥ .30, drop weak items) → internal consistency (α/ω ≥ .70 per face) → factor structure (CFA of six faces) → test–retest → measurement invariance across age groups, gender and home language → convergent validity (e.g. Emotional with an established EI measure) → norms (percentiles per programme).
* **Translation.** isiZulu and Afrikaans forms must follow the *ITC Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests* (2nd ed., 2017; *International Journal of Testing* 18(2)): forward and back translation by qualified bilinguals, cognitive interviews, then equivalence testing. **No machine translation into production.**
* **South African law.** Section 8 of the Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998 (as amended) prohibits psychological testing and similar assessments of employees unless the assessment is scientifically valid and reliable, can be applied fairly to all employees, and is not biased against any employee or group. Since the Employment Equity Amendment Act 4 of 2022 took effect on 1 January 2025, HPCSA *certification* is no longer required under the Act, but HPCSA *classification* of a test as psychological still restricts who may use it. **Rule: position Super-Cube® as a developmental self-reflection tool, never use it for selection or promotion decisions, and say so in reports.** POPIA ss 34–35 govern children's personal information (already handled by the guardian-consent flow).

## 2. Where Super-Cube® stood this morning (audit, 7 Oct 2026)

* 28 template items for Adults ("In my leadership practice, I consistently demonstrate: judgement."), all positively worded, one per skill. Kids: "I am good at: …". This is a self-label format; it invites acquiescence and social desirability and does not describe behaviour.
* No SJTs, no observer form, no Kids-specific wording, no norms. Attention, straight-lining and speed checks exist (stage 2).
* Sessions: templated Read → Engage → Apply with a self-rated "Quick check". No retrieval questions with feedback, no if–then plans, no spaced review, no capstone, no facilitator notes.
* Course intro videos (15 s) are real; session-level play icons were already hidden in stage 4.

## 3. Design rules for Super-Cube® (what the new content follows)

### Assessment
1. **Keep v1 intact as the standard form.** The 28-item instrument (a later version of the 18-item survey used in the DBA research) stays exactly as it is, labelled *v1 (standard form)*, so baseline and after-test results remain comparable. Pre and post always use the **same** instrument version.
2. **v2 behaviourally anchored Likert:** 6–8 items per face per form (Adults, Teens, Kids), each describing observable behaviour. About one-third reverse-keyed, written as positive descriptions of the opposite behaviour (no "not").
3. **Frequency scale** (Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Almost always) for v2, because behaviour is better reported as frequency, and it is the format children handle best.
4. **SJTs on every face, weighted towards Choices and Principles** (3 each; 2 for the other faces), using "most effective" instructions and a 1–4 expert effectiveness key per option. The face score blends Likert and SJT (70/30 by default) so neither dominates.
5. **Kids form:** reading age about 7–9, school/home situations, 6 items per face, at most one reverse-keyed item per face, picture-friendly SJTs with three options, and a parent/teacher observer form recommended.
6. **Observer/360 form:** parallel third-person wording for manager, peer and direct report (teacher/parent for Kids/Teens), at least 3 raters before a group is shown, self–other gaps on the report.
7. **Validity before interpretation:** keep the stage 2 attention item; add an honesty item in v2; keep straight-lining and speed flags. Flagged attempts are labelled, not hidden.
8. **Developmental, not diagnostic.** No clinical words, no selection use. Physical-face items describe habits, not health status.
9. **Faith-inclusive Spiritual face:** items about purpose, meaning, reflection, belonging and contribution "through faith, belief, values or practice that matters to me", so a learner of any faith or none can answer honestly.

### Learning
10. **Every session follows one arc:** Hook → Core idea → Real-world (African) example → Reflect → Micro-practice → If–then plan → 3-question retrieval check with feedback → Journal prompt. Same order every time, so learners know where they are.
11. **Spaced review across 21 days:** review checkpoints on about day 3, 7 and 14 that mix retrieval questions from earlier sessions, then a **capstone** that integrates all six faces before the day-21 after-test.
12. **Retrieval checks are real questions** (multiple choice, with feedback on every option), not self-ratings.
13. **Honest media:** no play buttons without media. Where audio/video would help, say "Audio version coming soon" and give rich text instead.
14. **Facilitator/coach guide on every session** (discussion questions, a 10–15 minute group activity, a safety note for minors) so schools and corporates can run it in a group.
15. **Strengths-first reports:** lead with what is working on each face, then one specific next step for the learner's band, and link to the session that builds it.
16. **Plain language:** Adults about grade 8–10 reading level, Teens about grade 6–8, Kids about grade 2–4. South African English spelling (organise, behaviour, programme, judgement).
17. **Inclusive and safe:** African and global examples from public record only; no invented statistics; no medical, diet or clinical advice; Physical sessions carry "check with a health professional" framing.
18. **Translation:** English first. New strings are flagged for native-speaker isiZulu and Afrikaans translation following the ITC guidelines; nothing machine-translated goes live.

### Report visuals (added scope)
19. Premium radar: face colours, pre vs post overlaid with a legend, per-face deltas in text (never colour alone), animated draw-in that respects reduced motion, a data table alternative, crisp vector output for print.
20. Frame-worthy certificate: A4 landscape vector PDF, the cube mark, learner name, programme, en-ZA date, mini before/after summary per face, programme author *Dr Craig R. Muller, DBA (UKZN)*, server-issued ID with a QR code to `/verify/{id}`, plus a LinkedIn-sized share image and an "Add to LinkedIn profile" link.
